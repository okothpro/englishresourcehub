English Language & Literature — GCSE / IGCSE / O-Level

Edexcel · AQA · Cambridge (CAIE)

Language: Reading

  • Identifying explicit vs. implicit meaning
  • Analysing language choices (word class, imagery, tone)
  • Comparing two unseen non-fiction texts
  • Structural analysis of a whole text

Language: Writing

  • Directed / transactional writing (letters, articles, speeches)
  • Descriptive writing from a picture prompt
  • Narrative writing with a clear voice and structure
  • Planning in 5 minutes, not 15

Literature: Prose & Drama

  • Building PEEL/PETAL paragraphs that argue, not describe
  • Context without info-dumping
  • Tracking a character or theme across a whole text
  • Extract-based questions vs. whole-text essays

Literature: Poetry

  • Unseen poetry: first read, then annotate
  • Comparing two poems on theme, form, and tone
  • Anthology poem recall without rote memorising quotes
  • Writing about form and structure, not just imagery

Exam-week priorities

  1. Learn command words cold — "explore," "compare," and "evaluate" each want a different answer shape.
  2. Time each paper section against its mark allocation before the real exam, not during it.
  3. Keep a short bank of flexible quotations per text rather than trying to memorise everything.

English Language & Literature — A-Level

Edexcel · AQA

Literature: Close Reading

  • Building a critical argument, not a summary
  • Integrating critical perspectives (feminist, Marxist, post-colonial)
  • Writing on unseen extracts under timed conditions
  • Comparative essays across genres and periods

Literature: Coursework (NEA)

  • Choosing a viable comparative question
  • Independent research and source evaluation
  • Structuring a 2,500–3,000 word argument
  • Referencing and avoiding over-reliance on secondary sources

Language: Analysis

  • Applying linguistic frameworks (lexis, semantics, pragmatics)
  • Discourse analysis of spoken and written data
  • Child language acquisition case studies
  • Language change over time

Language: Original Writing

  • Matching genre conventions deliberately, not by accident
  • Commentary: explaining choices with linguistic terminology
  • Drafting with audience and purpose fixed from the start

Exam-week priorities

  1. Practise essay plans under 5 minutes so the argument is set before writing begins.
  2. Keep critical terminology precise — vague labels cost marks at this level.
  3. Review examiner reports for patterns in what loses marks, not just what gains them.
